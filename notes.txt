Introduction, ironing out some details:

This project builds a risk engine for a portfolio spanning equities, Treasuries, gold, currencies, and a Government of Canada bond priced off a bootstrapped zero curve. 
It estimates daily Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall in three ways: 
    historical simulation, 
    a parametric (variance-covariance) model, 
    and Monte Carlo simulation of thousands of future market paths. 
Each method is then backtested over more than ten years of data with formal statistical tests (Kupiec and Christoffersen). 
Those tests check whether VaR breaches happen as often as predicted and whether they cluster in time. 
The engine also stress-tests the portfolio under historical crises and hypothetical shocks, with an extension that propagates shocks through a causal graph 
rather than using arbitrary scenarios. 
The result shows which risk models hold up in calm and volatile markets, why they fail when they do, and how much their estimates disagree when it matters most.
